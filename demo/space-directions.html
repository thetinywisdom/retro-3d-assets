CRT shelf: four directions

The pieces are the same in every direction: your four CRTs on top of a shelf unit, books and PS1 jewel cases inside it with text you can edit, and the PlayStation with its controller on the lowest shelf. What changes is how a visitor moves through it, which matters most on a phone. These are flat sketches, not the final look.

DIRECTION A

The wall, straight on

One fixed camera looking at the whole unit, like the gallery photo. Nothing to learn: tap a thing and the camera moves in to it.

How it works

  • Tap a TV: zoom in, its page becomes usable.
  • Tap a book or a CD: it slides out and turns to face you.
  • Phone: the unit is taller than the screen, so normal scrolling moves the camera down it.

Trade-offs

  • Good: simplest to use, fastest to build, reads at a glance.
  • Less good: it is a flat view, so the 3D only shows when something moves.
DIRECTION B

Scroll tour

Scrolling drives the camera along a planned path: it opens close on one lit TV, pulls back to show the stack, then travels down the shelves and ends at the PlayStation.

How it works

  • Each stop is a section of your site: intro, work, writing, contact.
  • A short caption sits beside each stop.
  • Phone: identical. Scrolling is the one gesture every phone user already knows.

Trade-offs

  • Good: most cinematic, tells a story, best on phones.
  • Less good: visitors follow your order; jumping straight to one item needs a small menu as well.
DIRECTION C

The room corner

A small piece of a room seen at an angle, like the second photo: wall, framed posters, a lamp, the shelf. Visitors can turn the view a little and look around.

How it works

  • Drag or swipe to turn about 40° each way.
  • Posters on the wall can be your own strips.
  • Tap anything to move in to it.

Trade-offs

  • Good: most atmosphere and personality.
  • Less good: most work to build, heaviest on phones, and swiping to turn competes with scrolling the page.
DIRECTION D MY PICK

Put the disc in

The shelf is the menu and the TV is the page. Pick a jewel case, the disc goes into the PlayStation, the lid closes, and that project plays on the big screen. Books open on the screen the same way.

How it works

  • One main TV shows content. The smaller TVs show previews of the other discs.
  • The TV's own buttons still work: channels step through discs.
  • Phone: the TV stays pinned at the top, the discs and books become a row you swipe under it.

Trade-offs

  • Good: every object has a job, the PlayStation earns its place, works well in a narrow phone screen.
  • Less good: the insert animation must stay short (about a second) or it gets tiring on repeat visits.

How to stack the TVs

Real sizes matter here: the 20-inch is 450 mm wide, the two 9-inch sets about 218 mm each, the 5-inch only 162 mm. Two 9-inch sets side by side almost exactly cover the top of the 20-inch.

Editing would work like the monitor does now: a short list in your HTML, for example one line per case with its title and cover image. No 3D tools needed.

Three things to decide

  1. Which direction? They also combine: my suggestion is the straight-on wall (A) as the layout with the disc mechanic (D) on top of it.
  2. Which PlayStation? You wrote SCPH-1000, which is the original grey 1994 console with the grey controller without sticks. The picture you sent is the small white PS one (SCPH-100) with a DualShock. Tell me which one you want.
  3. How many items? A rough count of cases and books lets me size the shelf. Around 8 to 12 cases and 6 to 10 books fills two shelves well.
